In construction estimating, the terms quantity takeoff and cost estimate are often used together. While they are closely connected, they are not the same process. A quantity takeoff determines what is needed and how much of it is needed. A cost estimate takes those quantities and determines what the project is expected to cost. Understanding the difference helps estimators build a more structured workflow, reduce errors, and make better-informed decisions throughout the estimating process.

## What Is a Quantity Takeoff?

A quantity takeoff is the process of reviewing construction drawings, specifications, and other project documents to identify the materials, components, and quantities required project.

- Number of light fixtures
- Number of switches and outlets
- Cable lengths
- Conduit quantities
- Other electrical components

## What Is a Cost Estimate?

A cost estimate takes the quantities identified during takeoff and assigns costs to them.

- Material costs
- Labor Cost
- Overhead
- Markup
- Waste or allowances

## What Actually Changes Between the Two?

The biggest change is the type of information being added.

During quantity takeoff, an estimator might determine that a project requires:

`450 m of cable`

During cost estimation, that quantity is combined with pricing information:

`450 m × $3.20/m = $1,440`

The quantity itself has not changed. What changes is the cost information attached to it.

This distinction becomes especially important when prices vary by supplier, location, specification, labor rate, or project conditions.
